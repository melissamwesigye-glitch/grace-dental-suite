Fibre and traditional bridges

A bridge replaces a missing tooth by attaching a new tooth to the teeth on either side of the gap. Grace Dental Suite offers two kinds.

A fibre bridge uses a strong fibre framework and tooth-coloured composite bonded to the neighbouring teeth. It’s conservative, needs no surgery, and is usually completed in one visit. A traditional bridge is made in a dental laboratory and cemented over prepared teeth for long-term strength.

Sometimes the right space has to be created first. If neighbouring teeth have drifted into a gap over the years, a short course of braces can move them back before the missing tooth is replaced.

A bridge may suit you if

  • You have one missing tooth and healthy teeth on either side
  • You’d like to avoid implant surgery
  • You want a natural-looking result quickly
  • An old bridge feels uncomfortable or looks unnatural and needs redoing

What happens at your appointment

  1. Assessment

    Dr Grace checks the gap, the neighbouring teeth and your bite, and explains which bridge suits you and what it costs.

  2. Preparation

    For a fibre bridge, the neighbouring teeth are gently prepared for bonding. For a traditional bridge, they are shaped and impressions are taken.

  3. Building the bridge

    A fibre bridge is built and bonded in the chair. A traditional bridge is made in the lab while you wear a temporary one.

  4. Final fit

    The bridge is fitted, shaped and polished, and your bite is checked.

Aftercare

A fibre bridge is strong, but not as strong as your natural teeth. Be mindful when biting into hard foods.

Brush twice a day, clean carefully under and around the bridge, and keep up with six-monthly check-ups and cleaning.

Questions

Is a fibre bridge done in one day?

Usually, yes. A fibre bridge is typically completed in a single visit, with an immediate result.

Fibre bridge or traditional bridge?

A fibre bridge is quicker and more conservative. A traditional bridge is stronger for heavy chewing but needs more tooth preparation and a lab. Dr Grace will recommend what suits your mouth.

Can a bridge that was done badly be fixed?

Often, yes. A poorly fitting bridge can be redone to correct its fit, shape and balance so it feels comfortable and looks natural.

Is a bridge better than an implant?

Neither is better for everyone. A bridge avoids surgery and is quicker; an implant doesn’t rely on the neighbouring teeth. If an implant would suit you better, Dr Grace will explain why and refer you.
