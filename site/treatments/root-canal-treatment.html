Root canal treatment, done gently

Root canal treatment saves a tooth whose inner nerve and blood supply (the pulp) has become infected or badly inflamed. The infected tissue is removed, the canals are cleaned and sealed, and the tooth is protected so you can keep it.

Its reputation is far worse than the reality. With modern anaesthetic and equipment, many patients say it feels much like having a filling.

Dr Grace uses the cordless FKG Rooter, an endodontic motor that gives precise, steady control of the instruments inside the canals. That means smoother cleaning and shaping, and a more comfortable visit for you.

Signs you may need a root canal

  • Pain from hot or cold that lingers after the food is gone
  • A throbbing toothache that wakes you at night
  • Pain when you bite down
  • Swelling, or a small pimple on the gum near a tooth
  • A tooth that has darkened after a knock

What happens at your appointment

  1. Diagnosis

    X-rays and simple tests show what’s happening. Dr Grace explains your options, including extraction, and the costs.

  2. Numbing

    The tooth is numbed and isolated with a thin rubber sheet to keep it clean and dry.

  3. Cleaning and shaping

    The infected pulp is removed and the canals are cleaned and shaped with the Rooter.

  4. Sealing

    The canals are filled and sealed and the tooth is closed. Back teeth usually need a crown afterwards to protect them.

Aftercare

Some tenderness when biting is normal for a few days and usually settles with over-the-counter pain relief.

Avoid chewing hard food on the tooth until it has its final filling or crown.

Questions

Does a root canal hurt?

The treatment is done under local anaesthetic, so it should feel similar to having a filling. The pain people dread is usually the infection beforehand, which the treatment relieves.

How many visits will I need?

Many root canals are finished in one or two visits, depending on the tooth and how much infection there is.

Why not just take the tooth out?

Keeping your own tooth is usually best for chewing and for the teeth around it. Extraction is an option, and Dr Grace will explain both honestly so you can choose.

Will I need a crown afterwards?

Back teeth usually do, because a root-treated tooth is more likely to crack. Front teeth sometimes only need a filling.
