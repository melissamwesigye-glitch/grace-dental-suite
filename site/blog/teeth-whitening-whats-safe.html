Teeth whitening: what’s safe and what isn’t

Shop kits, charcoal, social media hacks and professional whitening, compared honestly by a dental practice.

A brighter smile is one of the most common things people ask us about. There are plenty of options out there, and not all of them are kind to your teeth. Here’s an honest guide.

Why teeth change colour

Teeth stain from the outside (coffee, tea, red wine, curry, smoking) and darken from the inside with age, as enamel thins and the yellower layer underneath shows through. Some medicines and injuries can also change a tooth’s colour.

Professional whitening

Professional whitening lightens the natural colour of your teeth using a gel applied under a dentist’s supervision. Before whitening, your teeth and gums are checked, because whitening over decay or gum problems can be painful. Your gums are protected, and the strength is chosen for your teeth.

Shop kits

Over-the-counter strips and kits are weaker, so results are usually slower and milder. One-size trays can let gel leak onto your gums and irritate them. If you have sensitive teeth or any untreated decay, it’s best to get a check-up first.

Charcoal and “natural” hacks

Charcoal powders and toothpastes are abrasive. They can scrub off surface stains, but they can also wear away enamel. Thinner enamel lets more of the yellower layer show through, so teeth can look more yellow over time. Lemon juice and bicarbonate scrubs carry similar risks.

Whitening toothpaste

Whitening toothpastes mainly remove surface stains. They can help keep your teeth bright after whitening, but they won’t change your teeth’s natural shade.

Things whitening can’t do

  • It won’t change the colour of fillings, crowns or veneers
  • It isn’t recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Some deep stains respond poorly, and bonding may be a better option

Keeping your results

  • Go easy on strongly coloured food and drinks for 48 hours after whitening
  • Rinse with water after coffee or tea
  • Keep up your six-monthly check-ups and cleaning

Thinking about whitening? Start with a check-up. Dr Grace will tell you honestly what result to expect and which option suits your teeth.

This article is general information, not a diagnosis. For advice about your own teeth, book a check-up.
