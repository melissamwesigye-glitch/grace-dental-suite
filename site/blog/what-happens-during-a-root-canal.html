What really happens during a root canal

Root canals have a scary reputation. Here’s what actually happens at each step, how it feels, and how to recover well.

Say “root canal” and most people wince. The truth is that the treatment itself is usually no more uncomfortable than a filling. What hurts is the infection that leads to it, and a root canal is what takes that pain away.

Why a tooth needs a root canal

Inside every tooth is the pulp: soft tissue with nerves and blood vessels. Deep decay, a crack or a hard knock can let bacteria in. Once the pulp is infected or badly inflamed, it can’t heal by itself. Left alone, the infection can spread into the bone and cause an abscess.

A root canal removes that infected tissue and seals the tooth, so you keep your natural tooth instead of losing it.

The signs to look out for

  • Pain from hot or cold that lingers after the food or drink is gone
  • A throbbing toothache, especially one that wakes you at night
  • Pain when you bite
  • A swollen gum, or a small pimple on the gum near the tooth
  • A tooth that has turned darker after an injury

What happens, step by step

1. Finding the cause

An X-ray and a few simple tests show which tooth is the problem and how far the infection has gone. Before anything starts, Dr Grace explains your options, including taking the tooth out, and what each costs.

2. Numbing

The tooth is numbed with local anaesthetic. A thin rubber sheet is placed around it to keep it clean and dry. You’ll feel pressure but not pain.

3. Cleaning the canals

A small opening is made in the top of the tooth, and the infected pulp is removed. The narrow canals are then cleaned and shaped. At Grace Dental Suite this is done with the cordless FKG Rooter, an endodontic motor that gives precise, steady control. That precision makes the process smoother.

4. Sealing

The clean canals are filled and sealed so bacteria can’t get back in, and the opening is closed with a filling.

5. Protecting the tooth

A root-treated back tooth is more likely to crack, so it usually needs a crown afterwards. Front teeth sometimes only need a filling.

How long does it take?

Many root canals are finished in one or two visits, depending on the tooth and how much infection there is.

Recovering well

  • Expect some tenderness when biting for a few days. Over-the-counter pain relief usually helps.
  • Avoid chewing hard food on the tooth until it has its final filling or crown.
  • Call us if you have swelling, or if pain gets worse instead of better.

The bottom line: a root canal is a way to get out of pain and keep your tooth. If you’re nervous, tell us. We’ll explain every step before we do it and go at your pace.
